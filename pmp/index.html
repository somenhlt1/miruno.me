Before anyone signs, have a Virginia-licensed business attorney review it, and have a CPA review the tax sections (Articles 5 and 12). Laws change, and your attorney may recommend changes for your situation.
How to complete it
  1. Fill in the company details on this page and the members in Schedule A. Names typed there fill in the signature blocks automatically.
  2. Tick one box wherever you see a choice (management style, tie-breaker, vesting, and so on).
  3. Bring the printed draft to your attorney. After review, each member signs and dates the signature page, Exhibit 1 (initial resolutions) and, if married, Exhibit 2 (spousal consent).
  4. Keep the signed original with the company records. Give each member a copy. Banks usually ask for it when opening the business account.

This Operating Agreement (the “Agreement”) is made effective as of (the “Effective Date”) by and among the persons listed in Schedule A (each a “Member” and together the “Members”) and (the “Company”). The Members agree as follows:

Article 1 — Formation

1.1 Formation. The Company was formed as a Virginia limited liability company by filing Articles of Organization with the Virginia State Corporation Commission (the “SCC”) on , SCC ID number . The rights and duties of the Members are governed by this Agreement and the Virginia Limited Liability Company Act, Va. Code § 13.1-1000 et seq. (the “Act”). Where this Agreement and the Act differ, this Agreement controls to the extent the Act allows.

1.2 Name. The Company’s name is the name shown above. The Company may do business under other names approved by a Majority Vote, after filing any required assumed-name certificate.

1.3 Principal Office. The Company’s principal office is , or another place chosen by a Majority Vote.

1.4 Registered Agent. The Company’s registered agent is at .

1.5 Purpose. The Company’s purpose is to import, store, distribute, and sell food products, beverages, and related restaurant and grocery supplies, including products sourced from Vietnam, at wholesale and retail, and to engage in any other lawful business permitted under the Act.

1.6 Term. The Company began on the date its Articles of Organization were filed and continues until dissolved under Article 15.

1.7 No State-Law Partnership. The Members intend that the Company not be a partnership or joint venture for any purpose other than federal and state income tax, and no Member is a partner of another Member for any other purpose.

Article 2 — Definitions

  • Percentage Interest — each Member’s ownership share shown in Schedule A, as updated under this Agreement.
  • Majority Vote — approval of Members holding more than 50% of all Percentage Interests.
  • Supermajority Vote — approval of Members holding at least 75% of all Percentage Interests.
  • Unanimous Vote — approval of all Members, not counting a Member whose own conduct or interest is being voted on where this Agreement says so.
  • Capital Contribution — money or property a Member contributes to the Company in exchange for an ownership interest (not a loan).
  • Member Loan — money a Member lends to the Company under a written note, repaid before distributions to Members.
  • Triggering Event — an event listed in Section 11.1 that allows or requires the purchase of a Member’s interest.
  • Fair Value — the value of a Member’s interest determined under Section 11.4.

Article 3 — Members and Capital

3.1 Initial Contributions. Each Member will contribute the money, property, or services shown in Schedule A by the date shown there. Each Member’s Percentage Interest is shown in Schedule A.

3.2 Capital or Loan. Every amount a Member puts into the Company must be recorded in writing as either a Capital Contribution or a Member Loan at the time it is made. If not recorded, it is treated as a Capital Contribution.

3.3 Additional Capital. No Member is required to contribute more money unless approved by a Supermajority Vote, in which case each Member is asked to contribute in proportion to its Percentage Interest within days. If a Member does not contribute its share (a “Non-Contributing Member”), the contributing Members may choose either to:

  1. treat the amounts they cover as Member Loans to the Company bearing interest at per year; or
  2. treat those amounts as Capital Contributions and adjust all Percentage Interests in proportion to total Capital Contributions made.

3.4 No Interest; No Withdrawal. No Member earns interest on Capital Contributions or may withdraw capital, except as this Agreement provides.

3.5 Capital Accounts. The Company will keep a separate capital account for each Member under U.S. Treasury Regulations § 1.704-1(b), as administered by the Company’s CPA.

3.6 Limited Liability. No Member is personally liable for the debts, obligations, or liabilities of the Company solely because of being a Member, except as required by law or as that Member separately agrees in writing.

Article 4 — Allocations and Distributions

4.1 Profits and Losses. Profits, losses, and tax items are allocated among the Members according to their Percentage Interests, subject to any allocations the Company’s CPA determines are required by the Internal Revenue Code.

4.2 Distributions. Distributions of available cash are made only when approved by a Majority Vote, and always in proportion to Percentage Interests. Before any distribution, the Company must keep a cash reserve of at least and must have repaid any Member Loans then due.

4.3 Tax Distributions. To the extent cash is available after the reserve in Section 4.2, the Company will try to distribute to each Member, by each estimated-tax due date, enough cash to pay the estimated federal and state income tax on that Member’s share of Company taxable income, using an assumed combined rate of .

4.4 Limits. No distribution may be made if, after it, the Company could not pay its debts as they come due in the ordinary course, or its total assets would be less than its total liabilities, as provided in the Act. A Member who knowingly receives an improper distribution must return it.

Article 5 — Management

5.1 Management Style (choose one)

5.2 Ordinary Decisions. Day-to-day decisions within the approved budget may be made by the Member or Manager responsible for that area under Schedule B. Any other ordinary decision requires a Majority Vote.

5.3 Spending Limit. Any single expense or commitment over requires written approval (email is enough) of at least two Members, unless already approved as part of an annual budget.

5.4 Major Decisions. The following require a Supermajority Vote:

  1. placing any purchase order for inventory over , including any new shipping container;
  2. borrowing money, or granting any lien on Company assets, over ;
  3. signing any lease or contract lasting more than 12 months or worth more than ;
  4. hiring or firing employees, or setting pay for any Member;
  5. approving the annual budget;
  6. starting or settling a lawsuit over ;
  7. adding a new product line that requires new permits or licenses (for example, alcoholic beverages).

5.5 Unanimous Decisions. The following require a Unanimous Vote:

  1. amending this Agreement or the Articles of Organization;
  2. admitting a new Member or issuing any new ownership interest;
  3. selling all or substantially all Company assets, or merging, converting, or dissolving the Company;
  4. changing the Company’s tax classification (including an S-corporation election);
  5. asking any Member to sign a personal guarantee (see Article 9);
  6. any transaction between the Company and a Member, a Member’s relative, or a business a Member owns (the interested Member does not vote);
  7. changing a Member’s Percentage Interest other than as provided in Sections 3.3, 3.7, or Article 11.

5.6 Inventory Cash Rule. Unless approved by a Unanimous Vote, the Company will not order a new container of inventory until at least of the previous container has been sold and paid for by customers.

5.7 Officers and Titles. The Members may give titles (such as President, Treasurer, or Operations Manager) by Majority Vote. Titles do not expand authority beyond this Agreement. Initial roles are in Schedule B.

5.8 Banking. All Company money is kept in accounts in the Company’s name and never mixed with personal funds. Payments over the Section 5.3 spending limit require approval of two Members. All Members may view every Company account and record at any time.

5.9 Member Roles and Responsibilities

Each Member accepts the role below and is responsible for the listed duties. A Member may get help from others, but stays accountable for its area. Roles may be changed by a Majority Vote, recorded in the meeting notes and in Schedule B.

(a) President & Supplier Lead —

  • Find, vet, and visit (or video-inspect) suppliers in Vietnam; collect samples.
  • Negotiate prices, payment terms, and written supply contracts with quality, indemnity, and insurance terms.
  • Before each order, get proof of the supplier’s current FDA food facility registration, and FCE/SID filings for any canned or bottled product.
  • Arrange a pre-shipment quality inspection and confirm product labels meet U.S. rules before goods ship.
  • Place orders only after approval under Sections 5.4 and 5.6; confirm supplier bank details by phone before every payment.
  • Chair the monthly Member meeting and keep the agenda.

(b) Treasurer & Finance Lead —

  • Open and manage Company bank accounts; pay approved bills on time; never pay personal expenses.
  • Keep the books in the Company’s accounting software; send invoices and collect customer payments.
  • Share a monthly report: cash, sales, profit, inventory value, and amounts owed by each customer with days late.
  • Prepare the annual budget with the other Leads for approval.
  • Work with the CPA on tax returns, sales tax filings, K-1s, and tax distributions; pay the SCC annual fee.
  • Keep the Company’s official records under Article 12.

(c) Sales & Customer Lead —

  • Find and sign up restaurant and store customers; aim to line up buyers before each container arrives.
  • Set prices within the price list approved by Majority Vote; give no discounts beyond approved limits.
  • Apply the customer credit rule in Schedule B; stop deliveries to customers 30+ days late and report them to the Treasurer.
  • Take orders, handle customer questions and complaints, and track sales against monthly targets.
  • Manage samples, sales sheets, website, and marketing within budget.

(d) Operations & Compliance Lead —

  • Manage the customs broker, customs bond, ISF filing (at least 24 hours before loading), FDA prior notice, and duties.
  • Maintain the FSVP plan and supplier records; lead any recall or FDA/customs hold response.
  • Choose and manage the 3PL warehouse; schedule container pickup within free days; count inventory monthly; track expiry dates (first-expired, first-out).
  • Plan and run deliveries (rented trucks or freight); keep vehicles and insurance certificates in order.
  • Keep licenses, permits, and insurance current (Virginia Tax, local business license, VDACS, Section 13.3 insurance) and track all renewal deadlines.

5.10 Duties of All Members. Every Member will: attend monthly meetings; help with deliveries, warehouse work, and sales events as scheduled; follow the spending limits and approval rules; respond to other Members within 2 business days; give written notice before any planned absence of more than 2 weeks; and keep Company information confidential.

5.11 Backups. Each role has a backup Member named in Schedule B who covers that role during an absence and has access to its passwords, files, and contacts. No role’s key information may be held by only one Member.

5.12 Authority of Each Role. A Lead may act alone within its area only for matters within the approved budget and below the Section 5.3 spending limit. Anything larger follows Sections 5.3 through 5.6.

5.13 Accountability. If any Member does not perform its role or the duties in Section 5.10:

  1. Any two other Members may give written notice describing what is not being done.
  2. The Member has days to fix it, and the Members will meet to help.
  3. If not fixed, a Majority Vote of the other Members may reassign the role, and any pay for that role stops.
  4. If the failure continues for months after notice, a Unanimous Vote of the other Members may treat it as a Triggering Event under Section 11.1(g).

This process is for not doing the work. Fraud, theft, or serious misconduct is handled as removal for cause under Section 11.1(f).

Article 6 — Meetings and Voting

6.1 Regular Meetings. The Members will meet at least monthly to review sales, cash, inventory, and customer payments. Written notes will be kept and shared.

6.2 Special Meetings. Any Member may call a meeting with at least 3 days’ notice by email or text, or less if all Members agree. Members may attend by phone or video.

6.3 Quorum. Members holding more than 50% of Percentage Interests make a quorum.

6.4 Voting. Members vote by Percentage Interest. A vote may also be taken without a meeting by written consent (including email or electronic signature) signed by Members holding at least the required percentage.

Article 7 — Deadlock

7.1 Deadlock. A “Deadlock” exists when the Members cannot reach a required vote on the same matter at two meetings held at least 7 days apart (for example, a 2–2 split).

7.2 Steps to Break a Deadlock. The Members will take these steps in order:

  1. Meet and negotiate in good faith for at least 14 days.

Article 8 — Duties, Conflicts, and Competition

8.1 Standard of Conduct. Each Member will act in good faith and in the best interests of the Company, and will not engage in fraud, gross negligence, or willful misconduct.

8.2 Conflicts of Interest. Each Member will promptly disclose any personal interest in a Company transaction. Such transactions require approval under Section 5.5(f).

8.3 Business Opportunities. Any opportunity a Member learns of to import, distribute, or sell food, beverages, or restaurant supplies from Vietnam or Southeast Asia belongs to the Company first. The Member must offer it to the Company, which has 30 days to accept or decline.

8.4 Non-Competition. While a Member and for months after ceasing to be a Member, no Member will own, manage, or work for a business that imports or distributes the same types of products as the Company to customers in Virginia, Maryland, or the District of Columbia, without a Unanimous Vote of the other Members. Passive ownership of under 2% of a public company is allowed.

8.5 Non-Solicitation. For the same period, no Member will solicit the Company’s suppliers, customers, or employees to move their business away from the Company.

8.6 Confidentiality. Supplier names and prices, customer lists, pricing, and financial information are confidential. No Member will share them outside the Company except as needed for Company business or required by law. This duty continues after a Member leaves.

8.7 Time Commitment. Each Member will devote the time described in Schedule B. Members may have other jobs or businesses that do not breach Sections 8.3 through 8.5.

8.8 Compensation. No Member receives a salary or guaranteed payment unless approved under Section 5.4(d). Members are reimbursed for reasonable, documented business expenses approved under this Agreement.

Article 9 — Personal Guarantees

9.1 No Obligation. No Member is required to personally guarantee any Company debt, lease, or contract.

9.2 Approval and Cap. A Member may sign a personal guarantee for the Company only after a Unanimous Vote approving that specific guarantee, and no single guarantee may exceed without a separate Unanimous Vote.

9.3 Sharing the Risk. If a Member pays on an approved guarantee, (a) the Company will reimburse that Member first, and (b) if the Company cannot, each other Member will reimburse the paying Member its share in proportion to Percentage Interests, within 60 days of written demand. This applies whether or not the other Members also signed the guarantee.

9.4 Release on Exit. When a Member leaves, the Company and remaining Members will use best efforts to release that Member from any guarantees within 90 days, and will indemnify that Member for any later payment on them.

Article 10 — Transfers of Ownership

10.1 General Restriction. No Member may sell, give, pledge, or otherwise transfer any part of its interest without a Unanimous Vote of the other Members, except as provided in this Article and Article 11. A transfer that breaks this Article is void.

10.2 Right of First Refusal. A Member who receives a good-faith written offer from an outside buyer must first offer the interest to the Company, and then to the other Members in proportion to their Percentage Interests, on the same price and terms. They have 30 days to accept. If they decline, the selling Member may sell to the outside buyer within 60 days on terms no better for the buyer, but the buyer becomes a Member only with a Unanimous Vote.

10.3 Assignees. A person who receives an interest without being admitted as a Member receives only the right to share in distributions and has no vote or management rights.

10.4 Estate Planning. A Member may transfer its interest to a revocable trust it controls, if the Member keeps all voting rights and the trust agrees in writing to be bound by this Agreement.

Article 11 — Exit, Buyout, and Triggering Events

11.1 Triggering Events. Each of the following is a Triggering Event for the affected Member:

  1. Voluntary withdrawal — the Member gives at least 90 days’ written notice that it wants to leave;
  2. Death;
  3. Disability — the Member is unable to perform its duties under Schedule B for 180 days in a row;
  4. Bankruptcy of the Member, or a creditor seizing or attaching the Member’s interest;
  5. Divorce, if any part of the Member’s interest is awarded to a former spouse;
  6. Removal for cause — by Unanimous Vote of the other Members, for fraud, theft, a felony conviction, or a material breach of this Agreement not cured within 30 days of written notice;
  7. Not performing duties — as decided under Section 5.13(d).

11.2 Option to Buy. After a Triggering Event, the Company, and then the other Members in proportion to their Percentage Interests, have the option (and for death or disability, the obligation) to buy the affected Member’s entire interest at the purchase price in Section 11.4, by giving written notice within 90 days of learning of the event.

11.3 Insurance Funding. If the Company holds life or disability insurance on a Member, the proceeds will be used first to pay the purchase price for that Member’s interest.

11.4 Purchase Price (Fair Value). The price is the most recent Agreed Value signed by all Members in Schedule C, if signed within the last 12 months. Otherwise, the price is set by an independent business appraiser chosen by Majority Vote of the remaining Members, with the cost shared equally by the Company and the departing Member. For removal for cause, the price is of Fair Value. For not performing duties (Section 11.1(g)), the price is of Fair Value. For voluntary withdrawal within the first two years after the Effective Date, the price is of Fair Value.

11.5 Payment Terms. The buyer pays at least at closing and the rest in equal monthly payments over no more than months, with interest at the Applicable Federal Rate, under a promissory note secured by the purchased interest. Closing occurs within 60 days after the price is set.

11.6 Buy-Sell Offer (last resort). During a Deadlock under Section 7.2(c), any Member (the “Offeror”) may give written notice naming a price for 100% of the Company. Each other Member must, within 45 days, either sell its interest to the Offeror at that price (in proportion), or buy the Offeror’s interest at that price (in proportion). This encourages a fair price.

11.7 After Exit. A departing Member stops having any vote or management rights on the date of the Triggering Event, keeps its duties under Sections 8.4 through 8.6, and returns all Company property and records.

Article 12 — Books, Records, and Taxes

12.1 Records. The Company keeps complete books and records at its principal office or in shared cloud storage, including this Agreement, the Articles of Organization, tax returns, financial statements, meeting notes, supplier and customer contracts, import and FDA/FSVP records, and insurance policies. Every Member may inspect and copy them at any time.

12.2 Accounting. The fiscal year is the calendar year. The Company uses the method of accounting (as advised by the CPA) and accounting software chosen by Majority Vote.

12.3 Reports. The Treasurer (Schedule B) will share a monthly report of cash, sales, inventory, amounts owed by customers (with how many days late), and bills due.

12.4 Tax Classification. The Company will be taxed as a partnership for federal and state income tax purposes. It will file IRS Form 1065 and give each Member a Schedule K-1 within 75 days after year-end, or as soon as practical.

12.5 Partnership Representative. is designated the “partnership representative” under Internal Revenue Code § 6223, and must keep the other Members informed of any tax audit and may not settle one without a Majority Vote.

Article 13 — Liability Protection, Indemnification, and Insurance

13.1 Exculpation. No Member is liable to the Company or other Members for any act taken in good faith within the authority of this Agreement, except for fraud, gross negligence, willful misconduct, a knowing violation of law, or a breach of this Agreement.

13.2 Indemnification. The Company will indemnify and hold harmless each Member against losses, claims, and reasonable legal fees arising from Company business, except where Section 13.1 excludes protection. Indemnity is paid only from Company assets; no Member must contribute personally for it.

13.3 Required Insurance. The Company will maintain, as long as available on reasonable terms, at least: general and product liability insurance of $1,000,000 per occurrence and $2,000,000 aggregate; property/inventory insurance; marine cargo insurance for every shipment; and hired and non-owned auto coverage if Members drive for Company business.

13.4 Separateness. To protect the Members’ limited liability, the Company will keep its own bank accounts, sign contracts in its own name (“[Company], LLC, by [Name], Member”), keep proper records, and not pay Members’ personal expenses.

Article 14 — Regulatory Compliance

The Company will comply with all laws that apply to importing and selling food, including: U.S. Customs and Border Protection entry and bond requirements; U.S. Food and Drug Administration rules, including the Foreign Supplier Verification Program (FSVP), prior notice for each shipment, labeling, and confirming that each supplier holds a current FDA food facility registration; Virginia Department of Agriculture and Consumer Services (VDACS) requirements; Virginia tax registration; local business licenses; and trademark law (the Company will import branded products only from the brand owner or an authorized source). The Member named in Schedule B as Compliance Lead tracks these duties and deadlines.

Article 15 — Dissolution and Winding Up

15.1 Events. The Company dissolves only on (a) a Unanimous Vote; (b) a court order of dissolution under the Act; (c) the sale of all or substantially all its assets; or (d) any other event that requires dissolution under the Act that is not waived as the Act allows. The death, disability, bankruptcy, or withdrawal of a Member does not by itself dissolve the Company.

15.2 Winding Up. The Members (or a liquidator chosen by Majority Vote) will sell or collect Company assets and pay, in this order: (a) creditors other than Members, including taxes and customs duties; (b) Member Loans with interest; (c) a reserve for unknown liabilities; and (d) the remaining amount to the Members in proportion to their positive capital account balances. The Company will then file Articles of Cancellation with the SCC.

Article 16 — Disputes

16.1 Negotiation and Mediation. Members will first try to resolve any dispute about this Agreement by meeting in good faith for 30 days, and then by mediation in Fairfax County, Virginia.

16.2 Final Resolution (choose one).

16.3 Urgent Relief. Any Member may ask a court for an injunction to stop a breach of Article 8 or 10 while a dispute is pending.

Article 17 — General Provisions

17.1 Entire Agreement. This Agreement, with its Schedules and Exhibits, is the entire agreement of the Members about the Company and replaces all earlier discussions.

17.2 Amendments. This Agreement may be amended only in a writing signed by all Members.

17.3 Governing Law. Virginia law governs this Agreement.

17.4 Severability. If any part is held unenforceable, the rest stays in effect, and the unenforceable part is limited only as much as needed to make it enforceable.

17.5 Notices. Notices must be in writing and sent to the addresses or emails in Schedule A. Email notice is effective when sent, unless the sender receives a delivery failure.

17.6 Binding Effect. This Agreement binds and benefits the Members and their heirs, executors, and permitted successors.

17.7 Counterparts and E-Signatures. This Agreement may be signed in counterparts and by electronic signature, each of which is an original.

17.8 Independent Advice. Each Member confirms it has had the chance to review this Agreement with its own attorney and tax advisor, and is not relying on any other Member for legal or tax advice.
